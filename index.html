About Timelane

Timelane builds clean, slide-ready roadmaps you can paste straight into PowerPoint. Import a CSV or build one by hand, and export crisp images.

Your data is safe. Timelane runs entirely on your machine, in your browser — there's nothing to install, no account, no server and no third-party services. Nothing you type or import ever leaves your computer, and the page even works with no internet connection.

Sceptical? Good. Open your browser's developer tools, watch the Network tab, and build a roadmap — you'll see nothing sent.

Your plan is saved in your browser on this device so you don't lose work — use Clear all data and start fresh to remove it.

Guide

Quick start

  1. Get your data in. On the Data tab, paste a CSV and press Use this data, open a .csv file, or build the plan by hand with + Add item (Clear all and load sample restores the demo plan).
  2. Shape the slide. On the Slide tab, set a title, pick a theme, choose how many rows each lane shows before it folds, and toggle the today line.
  3. Export for PowerPoint. Click Export PNG (top right) — or open its ▾ menu for SVG and your CSV. The slide is a ready-made 16:9 picture.

Lane required — the horizontal band the item sits in Technology
Item required — a bar with a start and end ERP implementation
Start required 01/03/2026
End blank, or equal to Start, makes a milestone 18/12/2026
Status green, amber, red, blue or grey (the default) amber

Dates can be 01/03/2026, 2026-03-01, Mar 2026 or Q1 2026. Day first, always: 04/05/2026 is 4 May, never 5 April. A month or quarter counts as its first day in a Start and its last day in an End. Anything Timelane can't read produces a warning naming the row — never a silent guess.

Header row optional — with headers, column order doesn't matter.

Lanes & folding

Lanes appear in the order your data first mentions them. Items in the same lane that overlap in time stack into rows; milestones (a blank End, or an End equal to the Start) draw as diamonds on the lane baseline.

"+ n more items" means a lane has more rows than fit cleanly — the longest-running items stay visible and the rest fold into that strip. Adjust the limit with Rows shown per lane on the Slide tab.

Exporting

Everything lives under the Export PNG button (top right): click it to save the current slide as a PNG in one go, or open its ▾ menu for the rest.

This slide — PNG image, or SVG (editable in PowerPoint: insert the file, then right-click it → Convert to Shape for native, editable bars). Whole plan — Deck (.pptx) is coming soon. Your data — CSV, reopen or edit later.

Tick Transparent background in the menu to export with nothing behind the chart — a PNG with an alpha background, an SVG without its background rectangle — for pasting straight onto branded slide templates.
